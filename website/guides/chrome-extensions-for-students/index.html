5 useful Chrome extensions for students and research

Research moves between sources, notes, drafts, and feedback. Choose tools for those specific steps instead of installing a large bundle of study extensions.

By the Scrollout team at FarFlash Group Ltd. - October 20, 2026 - 3 min read

1. Zotero Connector - collect sources with citation details

Zotero Connector saves items from the browser into a Zotero library, with bibliographic metadata where supported. Use it when you find a paper or source you may cite later.

Save it into the relevant collection and check the author, title, date, and publication details. A saved citation still needs review; do not assume an automatically detected record is ready to submit unchanged.

2. SingleFile - keep a readable reference copy

SingleFile stores a page in one HTML file. It can be useful for offline reference when you want the page itself alongside your notes.

Keep the original source URL and access date in your research notes. A local copy is useful for revisiting content, but it does not replace attribution or permission to redistribute someone else’s work.

3. LanguageTool - review a draft’s wording

LanguageTool’s Chrome extension checks spelling, punctuation, and writing. Its free and Premium tiers offer different levels of correction.

Use suggestions as a review step, then decide what matches your intended meaning. Follow your school’s rules for writing assistance, and review the service’s data handling before using it with confidential research.

4. Scrollout - explain a visual point

Use Scrollout for a page layout, diagram in context, or visual question you need to discuss. Capture the whole page, crop to the relevant area, and add an arrow or short note. PNG, JPEG, and A4 PDF exports are included for free.

A screenshot is an image, so keep citation details separately and use the original text or an accessible document when classmates need searchable material. Screenshots stay local to your device; export before closing the temporary editor.

5. Bitwarden - manage the accounts around your work

Bitwarden brings saved logins into the browser. It can help when you move between learning platforms, library resources, and collaboration tools.

Learn the vault lock settings before using a shared computer. Follow your institution’s account requirements and avoid saving personal credentials into someone else’s browser profile.

Try the workflow on one assignment

Collect a source in Zotero, keep a reference copy when appropriate, write your own notes, review the draft, and capture a visual question only when an image will explain it better. Add tools gradually and remove ones you do not need.

The extensions have different account, pricing, and privacy models. Use each project’s official documentation before installing. For choosing a reference format, read screenshot vs. saved HTML vs. PDF.
